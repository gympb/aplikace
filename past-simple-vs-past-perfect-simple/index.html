Past simple × Past perfect simple

Čeština dřív měla předminulý čas („byl jsem přišel“). Dnes ho skoro nepoužívá a pořadí dějů vyjadřuje slovy už nebo předtím. Angličtina na to má samostatný čas. „Když jsem přišel, film už začal“ je When I arrived, the film had started. Past perfect je minulost před minulostí: stojíš v jednom bodě v minulosti a díváš se ještě dál zpátky.

Czech once had a pluperfect (“byl jsem přišel”). Today it is hardly used, and Czech shows the order of events with words like už or předtím. English has a separate tense for it. “Když jsem přišel, film už začal” is When I arrived, the film had started. The past perfect is the past before the past: you stand at one point in the past and look further back.

Zlaté pravidlo: Past perfect vždy potřebuje referenční bod v minulosti: jiný minulý děj nebo čas, ke kterému se vztahuje. Bez něj použij past simple, i když se to stalo hodně dávno.

Golden rule: The past perfect always needs a reference point in the past: another past action or time it relates to. Without one, use the past simple, even if it happened a long time ago.

V čem je rozdílWhat is the difference

Oba časy mluví o minulosti. Past simple vypráví, co se stalo, krok za krokem v pořadí, v jakém se to stalo. Past perfect potřebuješ, když stojíš v nějakém bodě v minulosti a vracíš se k něčemu, co proběhlo ještě předtím.

Both tenses talk about the past. The past simple tells what happened, step by step, in the order it happened. You need the past perfect when you stand at a point in the past and go back to something that happened even earlier.

Past simple

  • Děj v minulosti, často s určeným časem (včera, v roce 2019).
  • Řada dějů v pořadí, v jakém proběhly.
  • Hlavní linie příběhu.
  • Nepotřebuje žádný jiný minulý bod.
  • An action in the past, often with a stated time (yesterday, in 2019).
  • A series of actions in the order they happened.
  • The main line of a story.
  • Needs no other past point.

I got up, had breakfast and left.

Past perfect simple

  • Děj proběhl před jiným bodem v minulosti.
  • Vždy potřebuje referenční bod v minulosti (jiný děj nebo čas).
  • Z tohoto bodu se díváme zpět.
  • Vysvětluje příčiny, výsledky a pozadí.
  • The action happened before another point in the past.
  • It always needs a reference point in the past (another action or time).
  • We look back from that point.
  • It explains causes, results and background.

When I got to school, the lesson had started.

Teď × tehdy: spojení s present perfect

Now × then: the link to the present perfect

Present perfect se dívá zpět z přítomnosti. Past perfect dělá totéž, jen z bodu v minulosti. Celý obraz se posune o krok dozadu.

The present perfect looks back from the present. The past perfect does the same from a point in the past. The whole picture moves one step back.

Present perfectI can't get in. I've lost my keys. Teď nemůžu dovnitř.I can't get in now. Past perfectI couldn't get in. I had lost my keys. Tehdy (třeba včera večer) jsem nemohl dovnitř.Back then (say, last night) I couldn't get in.

Pozor: past perfect neznamená „dávno“

Careful: past perfect doesn't mean “long ago”

✗ I had been to Rome in 2010.   ✓ I went to Rome in 2010.

Když ve větě ani v kontextu není jiný minulý bod, ke kterému děj vztahuješ, použij past simple. Na tom, jak dávno se to stalo, nezáleží.

If there is no other past point in the sentence or context that the action relates to, use the past simple. How long ago it happened doesn't matter.

Pořadí dějů ≠ hotový děj

Order of events ≠ complete action

Mezi past simple a past perfect rozhoduje pořadí: stalo se to před jiným minulým bodem? Jestli byl děj v tom bodě hotový, nebo ještě probíhal, rozhodne později mezi past perfect simple a past perfect continuous.

The order decides between the past simple and the past perfect: did it happen before another past point? Whether the action was complete at that point, or still going on, will later decide between the past perfect simple and the past perfect continuous.

PP simpleBy six o'clock, I had written the essay. V šest byla esej hotová.At six the essay was finished. PP continuousI had been writing for three hours when the power went off. Psaní ještě probíhalo (to přijde později).The writing was still going on (coming later).

Rychlý test

Quick test

Zeptej se: Vyprávím děje v pořadí, jak šly za sebou? Pak použij past simple. Stojím v bodě v minulosti a vracím se k něčemu, co proběhlo ještě dřív? Pak použij past perfect.

Ask yourself: Am I telling the actions in the order they happened? Then use the past simple. Am I standing at a point in the past and going back to something even earlier? Then use the past perfect.

Jak se tvoříHow to form it

Past simple

podmět + sloveso -ed / 2. tvarsubject + verb-ed / past form

+I worked. She left.
−I didn't work. She didn't leave.
?Did you work? Did she leave?
↩Yes, I did. / No, she didn't.

Po did je sloveso v základním tvaru: Did you left? je chyba, správně Did you leave?

After did, the verb is in its base form: Did you left? is wrong; say Did you leave?

Past perfect simple

podmět + had + 3. tvarsubject + had + past participle

+I had ('d) finished. She had ('d) left.
−I hadn't finished. She hadn't left.
?Had you finished? Had she left?
↩Yes, I had. / No, she hadn't.

had je stejné pro všechny osoby. Žádné has.

had is the same for every person. No has.

'd = had, nebo would?

'd = had or would?

Pozná se podle slovesa za ním. I'd left (3. tvar) = I had left. I'd leave (základní tvar) = I would leave.

Look at the verb after it. I'd left (past participle) = I had left. I'd leave (base form) = I would leave.

1

Děj před jiným minulým dějemAn action before another past action

2

Příčina nebo výsledek v minulém boděA cause or result at a past moment

Kdy past perfect nepotřebuješ

When you don't need the past perfect

Když děje vyprávíš v pořadí, jak šly za sebou, nebo když pořadí jasně určuje before či after. After she ate / had eaten, she went to bed. Obojí je správně.

When you tell the actions in the order they happened, or when before or after makes the order clear. After she ate / had eaten, she went to bed. Both are correct.

U when ale na volbě záleží: When I arrived, she left. (Odešla, až jsem přišel.) × When I arrived, she had left. (Už byla pryč.)

With when, though, the choice matters: When I arrived, she left. (She left after I came.) × When I arrived, she had left. (She was already gone.)

Kde past perfect ještě potkáš

Where else you'll meet the past perfect

Nepřímá řeč: She said, “I've lost my phone.” → She said she had lost her phone.
Podmínkové věty 3. typu: If I had known, I would have helped you.

Reported speech: She said, “I've lost my phone.” → She said she had lost her phone.
Third conditional: If I had known, I would have helped you.

Signální slovaSignal words

Tato slova často napoví, který čas zvolit. Vždy se ale dívej na smysl celé věty a na to, jestli v ní je referenční bod v minulosti.

These words often tell you which tense to choose. Always check the meaning of the whole sentence, though, and whether there is a reference point in the past.

Kam s příslovci

Where the adverbs go

already, just, never stojí mezi had a příčestím: She had already left.
before stojí obvykle na konci: I had never seen it before.

already, just, never go between had and the participle: She had already left.
before usually goes at the end: I had never seen it before.

Příklady v páruExamples in pairs

Stejný obsah, jiný čas, jiné pořadí dějů. Vlevo vždy past simple, vpravo past perfect.

Same content, different tense, different order of events. Past simple on the left, past perfect on the right.

Jak se časy střídají v příběhuHow the tenses work together in a story

Hlavní linie příběhu běží v past simple. Past perfect se na chvíli vrátí zpátky a vysvětlí, co se stalo předtím.

The main line of the story runs in the past simple. The past perfect briefly goes back to explain what happened before.
