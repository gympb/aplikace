Past simple × Present perfect simple

Čeština má jen jeden minulý čas, a proto je tahle dvojice pro Čechy tak záludná. „Byl jsem v Londýně“ může být I was in London i I've been to London. Nerozhoduje, jestli je děj hotový. Rozhoduje, do jakého časového úseku děj zasadíš: do úseku, který už skončil, nebo do úseku, který sahá až do teď.

Czech has only one past tense, which is why this pair is so tricky for Czech speakers. “Byl jsem v Londýně” can be I was in London or I've been to London. What decides is not whether the action is complete. What decides is the time frame you put it in: a period that is over, or a period that reaches up to now.

Zlaté pravidlo: Když ve větě řekneš kdy se něco stalo (yesterday, in 2023, two days ago), použij past simple. Present perfect se s určeným minulým časem nikdy nepojí.

Golden rule: If you say when something happened (yesterday, in 2023, two days ago), use the past simple. The present perfect never goes with a finished past time expression.

V čem je rozdílWhat is the difference

Oba časy mluví o něčem, co se stalo v minulosti, a v obou může být děj hotový. Liší se tím, odkud se na děj díváš. Past simple ho umístí do minulého úseku, který je odříznutý od přítomnosti. Present perfect se dívá z přítomnosti a ptá se, co se stalo v úseku, který trvá až do teď.

Both tenses talk about something that happened in the past, and in both the action can be complete. The difference is where you look from. The past simple places the action in a past period that is cut off from the present. The present perfect looks from the present and asks what has happened in a period that continues up to now.

Past simple

  • Děj se odehrál v časovém úseku, který už skončil (včera, loni, v roce 2019, když jsem byl malý).
  • Víme, nebo je z kontextu jasné, kdy se to stalo.
  • Díváme se do minulosti, přítomnost nás nezajímá.
  • Typický čas pro vyprávění příběhů.
  • The action happened in a time period that is over (yesterday, last year, in 2019, when I was a child).
  • We know, or the context makes clear, when it happened.
  • We look back into the past; the present is not part of the picture.
  • The typical tense for telling stories.

I saw Jana yesterday.

Present perfect

  • Děj spadá do časového úseku, který sahá až do teď (dosud, v životě, dnes, tento týden).
  • Přesný čas neříkáme, není důležitý.
  • Díváme se z přítomnosti: zajímá nás výsledek, zkušenost nebo novinka.
  • Děj sám může být hotový (I've finished) i trvat dál (I've lived here for years).
  • The action falls in a time period that reaches up to now (so far, in my life, today, this week).
  • We don't say exactly when; the time isn't important.
  • We look from the present: the result, experience or news matters.
  • The action itself can be complete (I've finished) or still continue (I've lived here for years).

I have seen Jana today.

Pozor: hotový děj ≠ ukončený časový úsek

Careful: a complete action ≠ a finished time period

Rozlišuj dvě různé otázky. Skončil časový úsek? Podle toho volíš mezi past simple a present perfect. Je děj sám hotový? Podle toho budeš později volit mezi present perfect simple a present perfect continuous.

Keep two questions apart. Is the time period over? That decides between the past simple and the present perfect. Is the action itself complete? That will later decide between the present perfect simple and the present perfect continuous.

Past simpleI finished the letter yesterday. Hotový děj, úsek „včera“ skončil.Complete action, “yesterday” is over. Present perfectI've finished the letter. Hotový děj, díváme se z přítomnosti: dopis je teď hotový.Complete action, seen from now: the letter is ready now. PP continuousI've been writing it for an hour. Úsek sahá do teď a děj sám ještě probíhá (to přijde později).The period reaches now and the action is still going on (coming later).

Rychlý test

Quick test

Zeptej se: V jakém časovém úseku se to stalo, a skončil už? Pokud ano (včera, loni, v pondělí), použij past simple. Pokud úsek trvá do teď (v životě, dosud, dnes, tento týden), nebo pokud čas neříkáš a zajímá tě výsledek teď, použij present perfect.

Ask yourself: In which time period did it happen, and is that period over? If yes (yesterday, last year, on Monday), use the past simple. If the period continues up to now (in my life, so far, today, this week), or you don't say when and care about the result now, use the present perfect.

Past simple

podmět + sloveso -ed / 2. tvarsubject + verb-ed / past form

+I worked. She went.
−I didn't work. She didn't go.
?Did you work? Did she go?
↩Yes, I did. / No, she didn't.

V záporu a otázce pomáhá did a sloveso je zpět v základním tvaru: Did you went? je chyba, správně Did you go?

In negatives and questions, did does the work and the main verb goes back to its base form: Did you went? is wrong; say Did you go?

Sloveso be má vlastní tvary: I/he/she/it was, you/we/they were, otázka Were you…?

The verb be has its own forms: I/he/she/it was, you/we/they were, question Were you…?

Present perfect

podmět + have/has + 3. tvarsubject + have/has + past participle

+I have ('ve) worked. She has ('s) gone.
−I haven't worked. She hasn't gone.
?Have you worked? Has she gone?
↩Yes, I have. / No, she hasn't.

has jen ve 3. osobě jednotného čísla (he, she, it). Všude jinde have.

has only with he, she and it. Everywhere else, have.

Pozor na She's gone (= has gone) vs. She's going (= is going).

Watch out: She's gone (= has gone) vs. She's going (= is going).

been vs. gone

He has been to Paris. Byl tam a už se vrátil (zkušenost).He went there and came back (experience).
He has gone to Paris. Odjel tam a teď tam je.He went there and is there now.

for vs. since

for + délka trvání: for three years, for a week. since + začátek: since 2020, since Monday, since I was ten. Čeština obojí řekne slovem „už“ a přítomným časem: „Bydlím tu tři roky“ = I have lived here for three years, nikoli I live here for three years.

for + a length of time: for three years, for a week. since + a starting point: since 2020, since Monday, since I was ten. Czech uses the present tense here (“Bydlím tu tři roky”), but English needs the present perfect: I have lived here for three years, not I live here for three years.

Britská a americká angličtina

British and American English

V americké hovorové angličtině uslyšíš i Did you eat yet? nebo I just saw him. Ve škole a u zkoušek (Cambridge, maturita) se drž britského úzu: Have you eaten yet? I've just seen him.

In informal American English you'll also hear Did you eat yet? or I just saw him. At school and in exams (Cambridge, maturita), stick to British usage: Have you eaten yet? I've just seen him.

Kam s příslovci

Where the adverbs go

just, already, ever, never stojí mezi have a příčestím: I've already finished.
yet stojí na konci záporné věty nebo otázky: Have you finished yet? I haven't finished yet.

just, already, ever, never go between have and the participle: I've already finished.
yet goes at the end of negatives and questions: Have you finished yet? I haven't finished yet.

Jak se časy střídají v rozhovoruHow the tenses alternate in a conversation

Typický vzorec: otázka v present perfect otevře téma (zkušenost). Jakmile se ptáme na podrobnosti (kdy, s kým, jak), přepneme do past simple.

A typical pattern: a present perfect question opens the topic (experience). As soon as we ask for details (when, who with, how), we switch to the past simple.

PetraHave you ever been to Scotland?

TomYes, I have. I've been there twice.

PetraWhen did you go there?

TomThe last time was in 2023. I went with my brother.

PetraDid you see Loch Ness?

TomWe did, but we didn't see the monster!
